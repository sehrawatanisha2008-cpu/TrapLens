Scam Intelligence Database

Common online scam patterns users fall for.

TrapLens studies visible scam behaviors commonly found in fake websites, phishing attempts, deceptive online platforms, and manipulated digital experiences.

Fake Internship Scam

  • Promises unrealistic salaries
  • Asks for registration/payment fees
  • Uses urgency like “limited slots”
  • No verified company information

Fake Shopping Website

  • Huge discounts on every item
  • Recently created suspicious domains
  • Missing customer support details
  • Fake reviews and countdown timers

Phishing Login Pages

  • Imitates trusted platforms
  • Requests passwords immediately
  • Suspicious URL variations
  • Fake “verify your account” warnings

UPI / Payment Scam

  • Fake QR payment pages
  • Requests OTPs or PINs
  • Fake refund claims
  • Pressure tactics and urgency

Crypto Giveaway Scam

  • Promises free crypto rewards
  • Uses celebrity impersonation
  • Asks users to send money first
  • Fake livestream promotions

Social Media Verification Scam

  • Fake blue tick verification forms
  • Requests passwords or OTPs
  • Uses panic messages
  • Threatens account suspension

Why Scam Awareness Matters

Most online scams succeed because they manipulate trust, urgency, fear, rewards, or curiosity rather than using advanced hacking.

Human Psychology in Scams

Scammers often rely on emotional reactions before users have time to think critically or verify authenticity.
